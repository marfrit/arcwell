######## THE CONTROL ########
The cell is able to fail. A 4 KiB batch is latency-bound and must come out far
slower in GB/s than a slot-sized one; within 2x would mean the harness is timing
queue bookkeeping rather than bytes on the wire, and it reports RESULT=VOID.
Both cards cleared it (14.6x, 5.0x).

######## WHAT THIS SETTLES ########
- The endpoint link registers are junk on this topology (both GPUs report Gen1 x1,
  ~250 MB/s). The CPU root-port reading was right: B60 on Gen4 x8, A770 on a
  chipset-attached Gen3 x4. 13.99 GB/s at 89% of theoretical confirms it.
- **The A770 is ineligible for this campaign and the number says why.** At
  1.84 GB/s its host-to-device ceiling is BELOW the NVMe's own ~2.3 GB/s raw read
  (design-qwen-flash-next.md:1022). Nothing about a miss tier can be separated on
  that card: every path into it is capped under the drive's supply rate.
- The B60 is drive-bound, not fabric-bound: 13.99 GB/s of link against one NVMe's
  ~2.3 GB/s. A single drive can use at most ~16% of that card's bus.

######## AN OPEN QUESTION, NOT A CLAIM ########
arcint's recorded miss-feed rate is 1.68 GiB/s = 1.80 GB/s "in-container through
ZFS" (design-qwen-flash-next.md:1022). The A770's measured fabric ceiling is
1.84 GB/s. Those are within 2%. The recorded figure is described as a filesystem
read rate, which would not involve the GPU at all, so this may be coincidence —
but if any part of that measurement ran through the A770, it priced the bus and
attributed it to the drive. Worth one cell to separate; not asserted here.
